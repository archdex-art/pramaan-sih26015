Reading the drawings

Amber — judgementThe pure reconciliation engine, the weighted aggregation, and the detectability gate that governs what the engine is even allowed to consider.

Teal — external data in motionSTAC queries, OGC requests and COG range reads against sources that are open and free today.

Clay — the negative pathA failed gate, the mandatory dissent panel, and every conclusion the system declines to reach.

Dashed — not built for the hackathonThe offline field client, and the government integration that requires DoLR authorisation.

Four decisions these drawings encode

The core has no IO
The engine is a pure function called by the API, drawn deliberately with only two arrows: a bundle in, a verdict out. No database handle, no clock, no network call. The same bundle always produces the same verdict — the only reason a machine-generated conclusion can be defended under audit years after it was issued.
One arrow into the government workflow
PRAMAAN inserts itself between geo-tag and moderate in DoLR’s existing chain, and touches nothing else. It adopts the government’s own unique_id, its role vocabulary — SLNA, WCDC, PIA, WDT — and its work-status ladder. SRISHTI, DRISHTI and Bhuvan keep doing what they already do well.
Independence is what the weights buy
Terrain, satellite and temporal evidence are each generated without reference to the photograph. That is what makes their agreement mean something, and what lets the system degrade gracefully: lose a family and it says so, lowers its own ceiling, and carries on.
The system is allowed to not know
Inconclusive is the default outcome, not a failure state. A cloud-blocked season, a missing baseline, or a structure below the sensor’s detection limit all resolve to “requires physical verification” — never to an accusation. That phrase is the strongest language available anywhere in the interface, the API or the generated report.
